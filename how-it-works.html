How it works

The browser is the computer

Everything happens inside this tab. Your photos are never uploaded. Netlify only sends the files; your own browser does the computer vision and keeps your library.

📷 Your photo→Web Worker runs OpenCV.js on WebAssembly→Silhouette + score→Saved in IndexedDB
The eyes

OpenCV.js

OpenCV is the most widely used computer vision library. OpenCV.js is the same library packaged for the web: one file, and every function waits on an object called cv.

The muscle

WebAssembly

OpenCV is written in C++. It was compiled to WebAssembly, a compact binary format that runs at close to native speed inside the browser’s security sandbox. That is why the analysis takes only a fraction of a second.

The memory

IndexedDB

A real database built into your browser. It stores your reference shapes and every practice attempt (photos included) on your own computer, so they are still here after a refresh.

What OpenCV.js does with your photo

  1. Grayscale and blur (cvtColor, GaussianBlur) so colour and noise don’t matter.
  2. Threshold (threshold with Otsu’s method) splits the picture into “dancer” and “wall”. Otsu picks the cut automatically; switch to Manual to choose it yourself.
  3. Clean up (morphologyEx) closes small gaps and removes specks.
  4. Find the outline (findContours). The largest outline is you, or the two of you in a dip. Optional grabCut refines the edges on busy walls.
  5. Measure (boundingRect, convexHull, contourArea): height ÷ width, and how “open” the shape is.
  6. Compare (matchShapes) with your reference. It uses Hu moments, seven numbers that describe a shape whatever its size or position. They become the match score, together with the proportions and openness.

Why it never freezes

All of this runs in a Web Worker, a background thread. The page sends the photo, keeps responding, and gets the answer back. If a photo ever takes more than 30 seconds, the app restarts the worker for you.

WebAssembly has its own block of memory that JavaScript’s garbage collector does not clean. Every image inside OpenCV.js is a cv.Mat, and the app frees each one with .delete() as soon as it is done. The result panel shows “Mats still in memory: 0” to prove it.

What it can and can’t see

Edges are clues, not understanding. OpenCV compares the outline of your whole silhouette. It does not find elbows, knees or hips; that needs deep-learning pose models, which are a different tool. For a couple, both dancers merge into one shape, which is useful for a dip because the shape of the dip is what you are practising. For the best results, use a plain wall, contrasting clothes, and keep the whole body in the frame.

If the app gets stuck

Use Reset app data at the bottom of the app. If the page itself is frozen: right-click → Inspect → Application → IndexedDB → salsashapes to look inside, or Storage → Clear site data to start fresh.
