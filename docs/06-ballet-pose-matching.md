# 06 · The use case: matching ballet poses

**Idea:** ballet is built on held, named positions with a clear **line**. A dancer saves a reference photo of a pose (from a teacher, a book, or their own best attempt) and practises towards it. Each attempt gets a score, a coach tip, a place in their history, and the app also **recognises** which pose in the library the photo looks most like.

## The 16 built-in poses
| Group | Poses |
|---|---|
| Extensions and balances | Arabesque · Penché · Attitude derrière · Attitude devant · Développé devant · À la seconde · Écarté · Retiré |
| Positions and pliés | First position, bras bas · Fifth position, en haut · Second position plié · Tendu devant · Tendu à la seconde · Fourth position croisé |
| Jumps and bows | Grand jeté · Révérence |

Each has a reference photo and an attempt photo in `samples/` (`ballet-<pose>-reference|attempt.jpg`, generated by `tests/make_samples.py`).

## Why silhouettes fit ballet
- A ballet position is mostly about **line**: how high the working leg is, where the arms are, how the torso tilts. An outline captures that well.
- Both silhouettes are scaled to the same height and aligned on their centre of mass, so where you stand and how far the camera is don't matter. The overlap stays sensitive to where the limbs point.
- **What a 2-D silhouette cannot tell apart:** front vs side (développé devant and à la seconde look alike side-on, which is why their arms differ in the samples), turnout, hip placement, épaulement, pointed vs flexed feet at a distance.

## Recognition results on the samples (tests/pipeline_check.py)
All 16 attempts are recognised as their own pose. Closest look-alikes (a dancer would agree): first ↔ fifth position (legs together), tendu devant ↔ tendu à la seconde, retiré ↔ fifth en haut (same arms), écarté ↔ attitude devant. The own pose still wins by ≥ 18 points.

## Photo guidance shown to users
- Plain light wall; dark leotard and tights (or the reverse). Whole body in frame, including pointed feet.
- Camera at hip height, side-on to the line you are showing, same distance each time. Use the self-timer (3/5/10 s).

## Coach messages (Margot)
| Score | Message |
|---|---|
| ≥ 85 | "Brava! That is very close to your reference." |
| 70–84 | "Close. The line is there; refine the details." |
| 50–69 | "Recognisable. Keep working on this position." |
| < 50 | "Quite a different position. Check the reference you picked, and that your whole body is in the frame." |
Plus one tip from the measurements: taller/narrower → "extend the working leg and the arms further out"; lower/wider → "pull up through the standing leg and lengthen the spine"; more compact → "extend fully through the working leg and point the foot"; more spread → "bring the arms back into their rounded position".

## Limits (said plainly in the app)
No joint detection; lighting matters; the score measures outline similarity, not turnout, placement, épaulement or musicality.
